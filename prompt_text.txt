## PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Upgrade SolarShare from a household-focused peer-to-peer energy trading simulator to a grid-connected P2P energy marketplace.

The key business rule is:

**Only the seller needs a solar installation to generate and sell solar surplus. A buyer can be an ordinary household, apartment, shop, office, factory, or other eligible grid-connected consumer without any solar panels.**

The seller exports surplus energy into the shared electricity grid. The buyer obtains electricity through their existing grid connection. SolarShare matches their energy quantities contractually and calculates the associated financial settlement.

Support buyers and sellers in different network zones, including distant participants, provided the simulated network connection, transfer capacity, and configurable trading rules permit the transaction.

Do not claim that specific electrons travel directly from a particular seller to a particular buyer. The system must distinguish commercial energy allocation from physical electricity flow.

## PHASE 2 — USER TYPES AND PARTICIPANT ELIGIBILITY

Support these participant types:

A. Solar Prosumer / Seller
- Has a solar installation.
- Has a generation profile and household or business consumption profile.
- Can sell only the available surplus after accounting for their own consumption and any configured storage behavior.
- Can view generation, self-consumption, exported energy, matched energy, unmatched surplus, earnings, and transaction history.

B. Grid Consumer / Buyer
- Does not need a solar panel.
- Has an electricity connection, consumption profile, and energy demand.
- Can purchase eligible energy through the marketplace.
- Must be able to register, log in, view demand, view offers, see estimated prices, view matched energy, and inspect bills and savings.
- Must never be rejected from the buyer role merely because they do not own solar panels.

C. Optional Prosumer-Consumer
- Has solar generation and household demand.
- Automatically consumes generated power first according to the configured model.
- May buy additional energy when demand exceeds available generation.
- May sell surplus energy when generation exceeds consumption.

D. Administrator / Grid Operator Simulation Role
- Can inspect network zones, participant status, simulated grid capacity, trading limits, energy losses, charges, market clearing, settlements, and audit records.
- Can configure simulation assumptions without bypassing normal authorization.

Keep role permissions enforced by the backend, not just hidden frontend buttons.

## PHASE 3 — ENERGY BALANCE AND SMART METER MODEL

Implement or extend an hourly energy-balance model.

For each solar participant:

Available surplus = max(0, Solar Generation - Local Consumption - Other Configured Local Use)

For each consumer:

Energy deficit = max(0, Required Consumption - Available Local Generation)

Use kWh for energy quantities and kW for instantaneous power or network capacity. Do not mix these units.

Create or extend simulated meter records containing:
- Participant ID.
- Timestamp or simulation interval.
- Generation, where applicable.
- Consumption.
- Available export.
- Grid import.
- Meter status.
- Measurement source: SIMULATED or VERIFIED, as appropriate.

Do not invent verified meter readings. All generated example readings must be labelled as simulated.

Prevent selling more energy than available surplus and buying more than eligible demand or configured limits.

## PHASE 4 — GRID-CONNECTED TRADING AND DISTANT PARTICIPANTS

Introduce a configurable grid-network abstraction that supports multiple connected zones.

Each participant should have:
- A grid connection status.
- A network zone or node.
- An eligible trading status.
- A simulated network capacity constraint.

Each eligible network connection or route should support:
- Origin zone and destination zone.
- Connection status.
- Transfer capacity.
- Configurable delivery-loss percentage.
- Configurable network or wheeling charge per kWh.
- Applicable trading restrictions.

These values are simulator assumptions, not claims about actual grid infrastructure.

Support:
1. Seller and buyer in the same zone.
2. Seller and buyer in different zones.
3. Distant trading between connected zones.
4. Rejection or deferral of transactions when a route is disconnected, capacity is exhausted, or a configured rule prohibits the trade.
5. Multiple transactions competing for limited network capacity.
6. Partial fills when only part of a requested quantity can be accommodated.

Maintain a clear distinction between:
- Seller's metered energy injection.
- Energy allocated to a matched trade.
- Energy delivered or allocated to the buyer after modeled losses.
- Residual energy handled by the configured grid settlement model.

For an initial simulation, if a route has a loss fraction L and the buyer must receive Q kWh, the required injection is Q / (1 - L). Reject invalid loss fractions and handle insufficient seller surplus.

Document the exact convention used. Do not silently deduct losses twice or claim physical power-flow calculations when the application only performs accounting simulation.

Use network-aware allocation or a documented deterministic capacity-allocation policy. Never promise unlimited long-distance delivery.

## PHASE 5 — AUTOMATED PRICING AND CDA

Preserve the existing automated market model.

- Calculate a demand-pressure-based recommended price using the current configured price floor and ceiling.
- Keep recommended prices distinct from seller asks, buyer bids, and executed clearing prices.
- Continue price-time priority for eligible orders.
- Preserve partial fills and remaining quantities.
- Apply the existing midpoint clearing rule only where the order-matching rules permit a trade.
- Enforce configured price limits and validate quantities.
- Keep manual bidding optional for advanced users; normal users must be able to rely on automatic recommendations and automatic order generation.
- Do not execute trades solely because two participants exist. Check price compatibility, energy availability, demand, network eligibility, and capacity.
- Avoid matching the same energy twice.
- Ensure a buyer without panels can submit demand and participate in the same marketplace as solar sellers.

If network charges or delivery losses are modeled, explicitly document whether they affect the clearing price, buyer charge, seller payout, or separate network settlement. Do not conceal charges inside the energy price.

## PHASE 6 — TRANSPARENT FINANCIAL SETTLEMENT

Preserve the configured utility benchmark of ₹8.00/kWh, feed-in benchmark of ₹2.00/kWh, and ₹0.20/kWh platform fee unless the existing configuration establishes different values.

For each transaction, calculate and display separately:
- Matched energy quantity.
- Seller's ask price.
- Buyer's bid price.
- Energy clearing price.
- Gross energy value.
- Modeled delivery losses.
- Network or wheeling charges.
- Platform fee.
- Seller net proceeds.
- Buyer's total energy and delivery charge.
- Comparable utility-bill baseline.
- Buyer's estimated savings.
- Settlement status.

Use one documented financial convention consistently. State whether the platform fee is paid by the seller, the buyer, or both. Do not count the same fee twice.

For example, if a seller asks ₹4/kWh and a buyer bids ₹6/kWh, the eligible midpoint clearing price is ₹5/kWh before any separately modeled charges.

If the platform fee is ₹0.20/kWh on settled trades, a 10 kWh trade generates ₹2 in gross platform-fee revenue. This is not net company profit.

Buyer savings must be calculated against an explicitly defined comparable baseline and must include applicable charges. Do not claim positive savings if the final buyer cost exceeds the comparison baseline.

Account for unfilled orders, canceled orders, invalid settlements, and failed settlement attempts.
